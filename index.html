NCERT Class 12 Chapter 12

Ecosystem — High-Yield Revision Summary

Structure, stratification, productivity metrics, decomposition stages, food chains, trophic energy transfer, and ecological pyramids.

1. Ecosystem Structure & Stratification

Definition: Functional unit of nature where living organisms interact among themselves and with the physical environment.

• Categories: Terrestrial (forest, grassland, desert) and Aquatic (pond, lake, wetland, river, estuary). Man-made: crop fields, aquaria.

• Species Composition: Identification and enumeration of plant and animal species of an ecosystem.

• Stratification: Vertical distribution of different species occupying different levels. E.g., trees occupy top vertical strata, shrubs the second, herbs and grasses the bottom layer.

Pond as a Functional Unit:

• Abiotic: Water, dissolved inorganic/organic substances, bottom soil deposit, solar radiation.

• Producers: Phytoplankton, algae, floating/submerged/marginal plants.

• Consumers: Zooplankton, free-swimming and benthic (bottom-dwelling) forms.

• Decomposers: Fungi, bacteria, and flagellates abundant at the pond floor.

2. Primary & Secondary Productivity Metrics

• Primary Production: Amount of biomass or organic matter produced per unit area over a time period by plants during photosynthesis ($g\,m^{-2}$ or $kcal\,m^{-2}$).

• Productivity: Rate of biomass production ($g\,m^{-2}\,yr^{-1}$ or $kcal\,m^{-2}\,yr^{-1}$).

• Gross Primary Productivity (GPP): Rate of production of organic matter during photosynthesis.

• Net Primary Productivity (NPP): Biomass left after respiratory utilization by plants: $\mathbf{NPP = GPP - R}$. Available biomass for heterotrophs.

• Secondary Productivity: Rate of formation of new organic matter by consumers.

• Biosphere NPP: Approximately 170 billion tons (dry weight). Land accounts for 115 billion tons; oceans account for only 55 billion tons (despite covering ~70% of Earth's surface, due to light limitation and nutrient deficiency).

3. Decomposition Process & Factors

Five Steps in Decomposition:

  1. Fragmentation: Detritivores (e.g. earthworms) break down detritus into smaller particles.
  2. Leaching: Water-soluble inorganic nutrients percolate into soil horizons and precipitate as unavailable salts.
  3. Catabolism: Bacterial and fungal enzymes degrade detritus into simpler inorganic substances.
  4. Humification: Leads to accumulation of dark-coloured amorphous substance called humus (colloidal, nutrient reservoir, highly resistant to microbial degradation).
  5. Mineralisation: Humus is further degraded by microbes to release inorganic nutrients ($CO_2, H_2O$, minerals).

Factors Regulating Rate:

• Chemical Composition: Slow if detritus is rich in lignin and chitin; rapid if rich in nitrogen and water-soluble sugars.

• Climatic Factors: Favoured by warm and moist environments; inhibited by low temperature and anaerobiosis.

4. Energy Flow & Ecological Pyramids

Parameter Details & NCERT Values Key Principles & Exceptions
Solar Energy & PAR < 50% of incident radiation is PAR (Photosynthetically Active Radiation). Plants capture only 2–10% of PAR (1–5% of incident solar radiation). Sustains entire living world; unidirectional flow obeying 1st and 2nd laws of thermodynamics.
Food Chains (GFC vs DFC) GFC (Grazing Food Chain): Producer → Herbivore → Carnivore. DFC (Detritus Food Chain): Begins with dead organic matter/detritus → Saprotrophs. In oceans/aquatic systems, GFC is the major energy conduit. In terrestrial systems, DFC transfers far more energy.
10% Lindeman's Law Only 10 percent of energy is transferred to each successive trophic level; 90% is lost as metabolic heat. Limits the number of trophic levels in a grazing food chain (usually 4–5 levels maximum).
Standing Crop vs State Standing crop: Mass of living material (biomass measured as dry weight) at a particular time. Standing state: Amount of mineral nutrients in soil. Dry weight biomass measurement is more accurate than fresh weight because fresh weight varies with water content.
Pyramid of Numbers Upright in grasslands (e.g. 5.84M plants support 3 top carnivores). Inverted/spindle in single large tree supporting herbivorous insects and birds. Tree ecosystem: 1 tree (producer) → hundreds of insects → birds.
Pyramid of Biomass Upright in grassland/forest (809 > 37 > 11 > 1.5 $kg\,m^{-2}$). Inverted in sea/lake: small standing crop of phytoplankton (4 $g\,m^{-2}$) supports large standing crop of zooplankton/fishes (21 $g\,m^{-2}$). Inverted due to rapid turnover rate and short lifespan of phytoplankton.
Pyramid of Energy Always upright, can never be inverted. (e.g. 10,000 J producer → 1,000 J primary consumer → 100 J secondary consumer → 10 J tertiary consumer). Energy is dissipated as heat at each trophic step obeying 2nd law of thermodynamics.
Pyramid Limitations 1. Ignores species functioning at multiple trophic levels (e.g. sparrow as seed eater and insect eater).
2. Assumes simple food chain; cannot accommodate food webs.
3. Excludes saprophytes/decomposers completely despite their vital role.		 Frequently tested in KCET & NEET exams.
